An epidemic curve shows measles cases over time, based on when each person’s rash began or when they were tested or reported to public health if rash onset is not available. Using these dates helps show who may have been infectious recently and whether the outbreak is growing or slowing. Because case reports often arrive after someone gets sick, these dates give a clearer picture of current community risk.  The Monday dates on the graph represent data from that date through Sunday of that week.

Community Transmission

The Department of Health reviews data from each county weekly to see if measles is spreading within the community. Community transmission means measles is spreading beyond a single household and is not tied to a case from another county. In short, it shows that measles is spreading locally. Individuals living or traveling to counties with community transmission may need to take extra steps, like catch up or early MMR vaccination.
